The European Accessibility Act & your Shopify store

What the EAA means for online stores, who must comply, and how to make your store accessible — without a risky overlay widget.

Updated September 2026 · ~7 min read

On June 28, 2025, the European Accessibility Act (EAA) came into force. For the first time, a broad EU-wide law requires online stores to be usable by people with disabilities — and it reaches far beyond Europe's borders. If you sell to customers in the EU, this affects you, wherever your business is based.

This guide explains, in plain language, what the law asks for, whether it applies to your Shopify store, the accessibility problems that trip up most stores, and the practical way to fix them.

What is the European Accessibility Act?

The European Accessibility Act (Directive 2019/882) is EU legislation that sets common accessibility requirements for a range of products and services — including e-commerce. Its goal is simple: people with disabilities should be able to browse, understand, and buy online just like everyone else.

Enforcement began on June 28, 2025. Each EU member state sets its own penalties, which the directive says must be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive." In practice that means the risk isn't just fines — it's complaints, forced remediation, and lost customers.

Does it apply to my store?

Most likely, yes. The EAA applies to any business that offers products or services to consumers in the EU — including businesses based outside the EU. A store in the US, UK, Brazil, or anywhere else that ships to EU customers falls within scope.

There is one narrow exemption: microenterprises that provide services and have fewer than 10 employees and an annual turnover under €2 million. Even if you qualify, accessibility is still worth doing — roughly one in four adults lives with some form of disability, so an inaccessible store quietly turns away real customers every day.

Quick check: Do you ship to any EU country, and do you have 10+ employees or more than €2M in annual turnover? If yes, the EAA applies to your store.

What the EAA requires

The EAA doesn't invent a brand-new technical standard. In practice, compliance is measured against WCAG 2.1 Level AA (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines), aligned with the EU standard EN 301 549. WCAG is built on four principles — content must be Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust.

For an online store, that translates into concrete things like:

  • Images (especially product images) have meaningful alternative text so screen readers can describe them.
  • Text has enough color contrast to be readable by people with low vision.
  • Form fields (search, newsletter, checkout) have proper labels.
  • Links and buttons have descriptive text — not "click here."
  • The page declares its language so assistive tech reads it correctly.
  • The whole flow — from product page to checkout and support — is operable with a keyboard and a screen reader.

The most common accessibility issues on Shopify stores

Shopify itself is a solid, modern platform — but accessibility depends on your content and theme, not just the platform. In practice, these five issues account for the majority of problems we see:

  1. Product images with no alt text. This is the single most common failure. Every product photo without a description is invisible to a shopper using a screen reader.
  2. Low color contrast. Trendy light-gray text on white looks elegant but fails WCAG contrast ratios.
  3. Unlabeled form fields. A newsletter box or search field with only a placeholder — and no real label — is hard to use with assistive tech.
  4. Vague link text. Dozens of links that all say "click here" or "read more" give no context out of order.
  5. Missing language declaration. When the page doesn't declare its language, screen readers can mispronounce everything.

The good news: most of these are fixable without redesigning your store.

Why accessibility overlay widgets don't fix it

You've probably seen the little accessibility "widgets" that float in the corner of some sites, promising instant compliance. Be careful. These overlays sit on top of your site and try to patch accessibility with JavaScript — but they don't fix the underlying content, are widely criticized by the disability community, and have been named in a growing number of accessibility lawsuits. Regulators and screen-reader users can tell the difference between a real fix and a cosmetic one.

Real compliance comes from fixing the actual content of your store — the alt text, the labels, the contrast — not from bolting a widget on top.

How to actually become compliant

Here's a practical path, in order:

  1. Audit your store against WCAG 2.1 AA to see exactly what's failing.
  2. Add alternative text to every product image, describing what the image actually shows.
  3. Fix color contrast on text that fails the ratio.
  4. Label your form fields and rewrite vague links.
  5. Declare your page language.
  6. Re-check after every change — accessibility isn't one-and-done, because new products and pages can reintroduce problems.

You can do all of this by hand. It's tedious — writing accurate alt text for hundreds of products is the slow part — but it's the right way.

FAQ

When did the EAA take effect?

June 28, 2025. Enforcement is now active across EU member states.

I'm not in the EU. Does it still apply?

If you sell or offer to sell to consumers in the EU, yes — the law applies regardless of where your business is located.

What standard do I need to meet?

WCAG 2.1 Level AA is the practical benchmark, aligned with the EU standard EN 301 549.

Are accessibility overlays enough?

No. Overlays don't fix the underlying content and are frequently criticized and litigated. Real fixes to your store's content are what count.

This guide is general information about the European Accessibility Act, not legal advice. Penalties and enforcement vary by EU member state; consult a qualified professional for advice specific to your business.
